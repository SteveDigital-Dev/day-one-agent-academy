# Plot Structure

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the five parts of plot structure
- Understand how conflict drives a story
- Map the plot of a story using a plot diagram

## The Five Parts of Plot

**1. Exposition (Beginning)**
Introduces characters, setting, and the situation.

**2. Rising Action (Middle)**
Events build tension and develop the conflict.

**3. Climax (Turning Point)**
The most exciting or intense moment. The main conflict comes to a head.

**4. Falling Action (After the climax)**
Events wind down. The conflict begins to resolve.

**5. Resolution (Ending)**
The conflict is resolved. The story concludes.

## What Is Conflict?

**Conflict** is the problem or challenge in a story. Without conflict, there's no story.

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Character vs. Character** | Protagonist opposes another character | Harry vs. Voldemort |
| **Character vs. Self** | Internal struggle | A student decides whether to tell the truth |
| **Character vs. Nature** | Survival against natural forces | Stranded on a desert island |
| **Character vs. Society** | Protagonist challenges social norms | Standing up against unfair rules |

## Practice

Read this brief scenario and identify the plot elements:

"Emma studied for weeks for the science fair. The night before, her project was accidentally destroyed. She stayed up all night rebuilding it. On the day of the fair, her new version was even better than the original, and she won second place."

1. Exposition
2. Rising Action
3. Climax
4. Falling Action
5. Resolution

## Remember

- Every story has a problem (conflict) that must be resolved
- The climax is the turning point — things change after it
- Rising action builds; falling action resolves
- Not every story follows this exactly, but it's the foundation
