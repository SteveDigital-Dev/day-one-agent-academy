# Writing Conventions: Commas and End Marks

## Learning Objectives
- Use periods, question marks, and exclamation points correctly
- Apply commas in compound sentences and introductory elements
- Avoid comma splices and other common errors

## End Marks

| Mark | Use | Example |
|------|-----|---------|
| **Period (.)** | Statements and commands | "The library closes at 5." |
| **Question mark (?)** | Direct questions | "When is the test?" |
| **Exclamation mark (!)** | Strong emotion or surprise | "What a great idea!" |

Don't overuse exclamation marks — save them for real emphasis.

## Comma Rules

**Rule 1: Compound sentences**
Use a comma before the conjunction (and, but, or, so) when joining two complete sentences.
> "I finished my homework, but I still have reading to do."

**Rule 2: Introductory elements**
> "After lunch, we went back to class."
> "However, the results were different."
> "Running quickly, she caught the bus."

**Rule 3: Lists**
> "I need pencils, paper, erasers, and a ruler."

**Rule 4: Dates and addresses**
> "On March 15, 2024, the school held a fair."
> "She moved to Austin, Texas, last summer."

## Common Errors

**Comma splice:** Two sentences joined only by a comma.
> Wrong: "I like pizza, she likes pasta."
> Right: "I like pizza, **but** she likes pasta."
> Right: "I like pizza**;** she likes pasta."

**Missing comma after introductory element:**
> Wrong: "After the movie we got ice cream."
> Right: "After the movie**,** we got ice cream."

## Practice

Add commas where needed:

1. "When I was young I loved reading."
2. "The cat sat on the mat and the dog lay on the rug."
3. "She packed a sandwich some fruit and a bottle of water."
4. Fix the comma splice: "It was cold outside it was warm inside."

## Remember

- Periods end statements, question marks end questions, exclamation marks show strong feeling
- Use commas in compound sentences, after introductions, and in lists
- A comma alone cannot join two sentences (comma splice!)
