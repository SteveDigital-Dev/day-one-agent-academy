# Main Idea and Supporting Details

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the main idea of a paragraph or passage
- Distinguish between main ideas and supporting details
- Use topic sentences to find the main idea

## What Is the Main Idea?

The **main idea** is the most important point the author wants you to understand. It's what the whole paragraph or passage is mostly about.

## Finding the Main Idea

**Strategy 1: Look at the topic sentence**
Most paragraphs begin with a sentence that states the main idea directly.

> "**Learning a new language takes daily practice.** Studies show that students who study for 20 minutes each day progress faster than those who cram once a week. Consistent effort builds vocabulary and grammar skills over time."

The first sentence is the main idea — the rest supports it.

**Strategy 2: Ask "What do all the details have in common?"**

> "Bees make honey. They also pollinate flowers. Without bees, many crops would not grow."

What do these details share? They're all about why bees are important. Main idea: **Bees are important to nature and agriculture.**

## Main Idea vs. Supporting Details

| Main Idea | Supporting Details |
|-----------|-------------------|
| The big picture | Specific facts and examples |
| Answers "What is this about?" | Answers "How do I know?" |
| One per paragraph | Many per paragraph |

## Practice

Read this paragraph and answer the questions:

"Coral reefs support thousands of marine species. Fish lay their eggs among the coral branches. Tiny organisms called polyps build the reef structure over many years. Reefs also protect coastlines from storm damage."

1. What is the main idea?
2. List two supporting details.

## Remember

- The main idea is the "big picture" — everything else supports it
- Topic sentences often (but not always) appear at the beginning
- If you can remove a detail and the paragraph still makes sense, it's a supporting detail
