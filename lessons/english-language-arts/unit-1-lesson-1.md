# Parts of Speech: Nouns and Verbs

## Learning Objectives
- Identify nouns as people, places, things, or ideas
- Identify verbs as action words or state-of-being words
- Distinguish between common and proper nouns

## What Is a Noun?

A **noun** is a word that names a person, place, thing, or idea.

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| **Person** | teacher, Maria, doctor, mom |
| **Place** | school, library, New York, kitchen |
| **Thing** | book, pencil, tree, computer |
| **Idea** | freedom, happiness, courage, love |

**Proper nouns** name specific people, places, or things and are always capitalized: *Ms. Rodriguez*, *Central Park*, *Tuesday*.

**Common nouns** are general and are not capitalized: *teacher*, *park*, *day*.

## What Is a Verb?

A **verb** is a word that shows action or a state of being.

**Action verbs** tell what someone or something does:
> run, think, write, sing, study

**State-of-being verbs** (linking verbs) connect the subject to more information:
> am, is, are, was, were, seem, become, feel

> Example: The dog **barked**. (action)
> Example: She **is** a student. (state of being)

## Finding Nouns and Verbs

Every sentence needs at least one noun and one verb.

> "The **cat** **sat** on the **mat**."
> - cat = noun, sat = verb, mat = noun

## Practice

1. Circle the nouns: "Maria read a book in the garden."
2. Underline the verbs: "The bird flew over the mountain."
3. Write three common nouns and three proper nouns.
4. Write a sentence with at least two nouns and one verb.

## Remember

- Nouns name people, places, things, or ideas
- Verbs show action or state of being
- Proper nouns are always capitalized
- Every sentence needs a noun and a verb
