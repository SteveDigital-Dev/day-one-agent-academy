# Character Analysis

## Learning Objectives
- Identify character traits using direct and indirect evidence
- Distinguish between static and dynamic characters
- Track how a character changes throughout a story

## Direct vs. Indirect Characterization

**Direct:** The author tells you explicitly.
> "Marcus was generous and kind."

**Indirect:** The author shows you through actions, words, and thoughts.
> "Marcus gave his lunch to the hungry kid and shared his pencils with everyone at the table."

Indirect characterization requires you to **infer** the trait.

## Character Traits

| Trait | What It Means |
|-------|---------------|
| **Courageous** | Faces fear or danger |
| **Compassionate** | Cares about others' feelings |
| **Determined** | Doesn't give up easily |
| **Deceitful** | Lies or tricks others |
| **Loyal** | Stands by friends |

## Static vs. Dynamic Characters

**Static character:** Does not change throughout the story.
> A villain who is evil from start to finish.

**Dynamic character:** Changes because of events in the story.
> A shy student who becomes confident after performing in the school play.

## Tracking Character Change

Ask these questions:
1. How does the character feel at the **beginning**?
2. What **events** change them?
3. How do they feel or act **differently** at the end?

## Practice

Read: "At first, Sophie avoided speaking in class. When her partner got sick, she had to present their project alone. She was terrified but did it anyway. After receiving an A, she volunteered to present again the next week."

1. Identify Sophie's character trait.
2. Is she static or dynamic? Explain.
3. What event caused her change?

## Remember

- Characters are revealed through what they say, do, think, and how others react to them
- Dynamic characters change; static ones don't
- A character's traits explain *why* they do what they do
