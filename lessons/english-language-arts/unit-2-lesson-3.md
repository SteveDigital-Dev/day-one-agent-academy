# Point of View

## Learning Objectives
- Identify first person, second person, and third person narration
- Understand the difference between third person limited and third person omniscient
- Determine how point of view affects the reader's experience

## The Three Points of View

**First Person** — The narrator is a character in the story.
> "**I** walked into the room and noticed **my** book was missing."
- Uses: I, me, my, we, us
- You only know what the narrator knows

**Second Person** — The narrator speaks directly to the reader.
> "**You** walk into the room and notice **your** book is missing."
- Uses: you, your
- Rare in fiction, common in instructions

**Third Person** — The narrator is outside the story.
> "**She** walked into the room and noticed **her** book was missing."
- Uses: he, she, it, they, name

## Third Person: Limited vs. Omniscient

| Type | What the Narrator Knows | Example |
|------|------------------------|---------|
| **Limited** | One character's thoughts and feelings | "She wondered where the book had gone." |
| **Omniscient** | All characters' thoughts and feelings | "She wondered where it was. He knew exactly where it was." |

## Why Point of View Matters

Point of view controls how much information the reader gets and how close they feel to characters.

- First person → intimate, biased, limited knowledge
- Third limited → close to one character, but can describe them from outside
- Third omniscient → all-knowing, can jump between characters

## Practice

Identify the point of view:

1. "We ran as fast as we could, but the rain made the path slippery."
2. "The teacher didn't know that the students had planned a surprise."
3. "You open the door and see a dark hallway ahead."

## Remember

- First person = I/we (narrator is in the story)
- Second person = you (narrator talks to you)
- Third person = he/she/they (narrator is outside)
- Third person limited and omniscient differ in what the narrator knows
