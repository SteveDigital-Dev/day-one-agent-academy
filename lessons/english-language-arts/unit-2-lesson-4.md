# Theme in Literature

## Learning Objectives
- Define theme as the central message or lesson of a story
- Distinguish between topic and theme
- Support theme claims with evidence from the text

## What Is Theme?

**Theme** is the big idea or central message the author wants you to take away. It's what the story is *really* about — deeper than the plot.

> **Topic:** Friendship
> **Theme:** True friendship requires sacrifice and honesty.

Notice: a topic is one word. A theme is a complete statement.

## Finding Theme

Ask yourself:
1. What does the main character **learn**?
2. What **changes** by the end of the story?
3. What **message** does the author seem to be sending?

Common themes:
- Courage is not the absence of fear
- Actions have consequences
- Everyone needs a sense of belonging
- The truth always comes out

## Topic vs. Theme

| Topic | Theme |
|-------|-------|
| One word | A statement or lesson |
| "Honesty" | "Honesty builds trust, even when it's hard" |
| "War" | "War destroys more than buildings" |
| "Family" | "Family is who you choose, not just who you're born to" |

## Supporting Your Theme

Always back up your theme with evidence:
> Theme: "Courage means acting despite fear."
> Evidence: In chapter 3, the protagonist is terrified but still walks onto the stage to perform.

## Practice

Read: "The tortoise won the race not because he was faster, but because he never stopped moving forward. The hare gave up when he thought he had plenty of time."

1. What is the topic?
2. What is the theme? Write it as a complete sentence.
3. What evidence supports your theme?

## Remember

- Theme is the lesson, not the plot
- A story can have more than one theme
- Always support your theme with specific evidence
- Topic ≠ theme (one word vs. a statement)
