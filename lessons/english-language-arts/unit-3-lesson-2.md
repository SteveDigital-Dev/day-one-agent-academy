# Informative Writing

## Learning Objectives
- Write a clear thesis statement
- Organize ideas using a structured format
- Support claims with evidence and explanations

## What Is Informative Writing?

**Informative writing** teaches the reader about a topic. It presents facts, explains concepts, and shares knowledge without trying to persuade.

## The Thesis Statement

Your thesis is the **main point** of your entire piece. It usually appears at the end of the introduction.

> Weak: "This paper is about coral reefs."
> Strong: "Coral reefs are vital ecosystems that support marine biodiversity, protect coastlines, and contribute billions to the global economy."

A good thesis is **specific** and **arguable** (even in informative writing, it takes a position on what's most important).

## Organization Patterns

| Pattern | When to Use |
|---------|-------------|
| **Chronological** | Steps in a process, historical events |
| **Order of importance** | Most to least important (or vice versa) |
| **Compare/contrast** | Showing similarities and differences |
| **Cause/effect** | Explaining why something happens |

## The MEAL Plan for Paragraphs

Each body paragraph should follow MEAL:

- **M**ain idea — the topic sentence
- **E**vidence — facts, statistics, quotes
- **A**nalysis — explain what the evidence means
- **L**ink — connect back to the thesis

## Practice

Write an informative paragraph about the water cycle using the MEAL plan:
- Main idea: (your topic sentence)
- Evidence: (at least one fact)
- Analysis: (explain the fact)
- Link: (connect to your thesis)

## Remember

- Informative writing is about teaching, not persuading
- Every paragraph needs a clear topic sentence
- Evidence without analysis is just a list of facts
- Stay objective — use "I believe" sparingly if at all
