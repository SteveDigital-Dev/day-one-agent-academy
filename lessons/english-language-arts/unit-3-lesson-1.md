# Research Skills: Finding and Evaluating Sources

## Learning Objectives
- Identify reliable and unreliable sources
- Use the CRAAP test to evaluate sources
- Take effective research notes

## Why Source Quality Matters

Not all information is equal. A well-researched paper depends on **reliable** sources. Using poor sources weakens your argument and credibility.

## The CRAAP Test

Evaluate every source using these five criteria:

| Letter | Criterion | Question to Ask |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|
| **C** | Currency | When was this published? Is it up to date? |
| **R** | Relevance | Does it directly relate to my topic? |
| **A** | Authority | Who wrote it? Are they an expert? |
| **A** | Accuracy | Is it supported by evidence? Can I verify it? |
| **P** | Purpose | Why was this written? To inform, persuade, or sell? |

## Source Types

| Source | Reliability | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| Academic journals | High | In-depth, peer-reviewed research |
| Books by experts | High | Comprehensive background |
| News websites (major) | Medium-High | Current events, primary reporting |
| Blogs / personal sites | Low-Medium | Personal opinions, anecdotes |
| Wikipedia | Variable | Starting point, not final source |
| Social media | Low | Opinions, not facts |

## Taking Notes

1. **Write the full citation** before you forget where information came from
2. **Paraphrase** in your own words (don't copy-paste)
3. **Note the page number** for quotes
4. **Mark direct quotes** with quotation marks
5. **Record your own thoughts** separately from source material

## Remember

- Always verify before you trust
- The CRAAP test takes 2 minutes but saves hours of trouble
- Keep your notes and citations organized from the start
- When in doubt, find a better source
