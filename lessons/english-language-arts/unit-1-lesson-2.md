# Sentence Structure

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the four types of sentences
- Understand subjects and predicates
- Write complete, clear sentences

## The Four Sentence Types

| Type | Purpose | Example | Punctuation |
|------|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Declarative** | Makes a statement | The sky is blue. | Period |
| **Interrogative** | Asks a question | Where is my book? | Question mark |
| **Imperative** | Gives a command | Please close the door. | Period or exclamation |
| **Exclamatory** | Shows strong emotion | What a beautiful day! | Exclamation mark |

## Subject and Predicate

Every complete sentence has two parts:

**Subject:** who or what the sentence is about
**Predicate:** what the subject does or is

> "**The student** **completed the assignment**."
> - Subject: The student
> - Predicate: completed the assignment

**Simple subject:** the main noun (student)
**Simple predicate:** the main verb (completed)

## Complete Sentences

A complete sentence must have:
1. A subject
2. A predicate (verb)
3. Express a complete thought

**Run-on sentence:** Two sentences joined without punctuation.
> "I went to the store I bought milk."

Fix: "I went to the store**.** I bought milk**.**" or add a conjunction.

**Sentence fragment:** An incomplete sentence.
> "Running through the park." (no subject)
> "The big red dog." (no verb)

## Practice

1. Identify the type: "Don't forget your jacket!"
2. Find the subject and predicate: "The children played in the yard."
3. Fix this fragment: "After the rain stopped."
4. Fix this run-on: "She loves reading she goes to the library every week."

## Remember

- Every sentence needs a subject and a verb
- Different purposes need different punctuation
- Fragments and run-ons confuse readers — always check your sentences
