# Ecosystems and Biomes

## Learning Objectives
- Define ecosystem and identify its living and nonliving components
- Describe the major biomes of the world
- Explain how energy flows through ecosystems

## What Is an Ecosystem?

An **ecosystem** includes all the living organisms (biotic factors) in an area along with the nonliving factors (abiotic factors) they interact with.

| Biotic Factors | Abiotic Factors |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Plants, animals, fungi | Sunlight, temperature |
| Bacteria | Water, soil, air |

## Major Biomes

| Biome | Climate | Key Features |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Tropical Rainforest** | Hot, wet year-round | Highest biodiversity on Earth |
| **Desert** | Hot or cold, very dry | Sparse vegetation, extreme temperatures |
| **Grassland** | Moderate rain, seasonal | Dominated by grasses, few trees |
| **Temperate Forest** | Four seasons | Deciduous trees lose leaves in fall |
| **Tundra** | Very cold, little rain | Permafrost, no trees |
| **Aquatic (Marine/Freshwater)** | Varies | Covers 70% of Earth's surface |

## Energy Flow

Energy enters most ecosystems as **sunlight**. Producers capture it through photosynthesis, then pass it to consumers.

```
Sun → Producers → Primary Consumers → Secondary Consumers → Decomposers
                                      ↓
                              (energy lost as heat at each level)
```

**Decomposers** return nutrients to the soil, where producers can use them again — completing the cycle.

## Remember

- Ecosystems = biotic + abiotic factors working together
- Biomes are defined by climate and dominant organisms
- Energy flows through ecosystems; nutrients cycle
- Decomposers are the recyclers of every ecosystem
