# Force and Motion

## Learning Objectives
- Define force as a push or pull
- Understand how force affects motion
- Identify examples of balanced and unbalanced forces

## What Is Force?

A **force** is a push or a pull on an object. Forces can cause an object to:
- Start moving
- Stop moving
- Speed up
- Slow down
- Change direction

Force is measured in **newtons (N)**.

## How Force Affects Motion

**Balanced forces** cancel each other out. The object doesn't change its motion.
> A book sitting on a table: gravity pulls down, the table pushes up equally. The book stays still.

**Unbalanced forces** cause a change in motion.
> You push a ball: your force is stronger than friction. The ball rolls.

## Types of Force

| Force | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Gravity** | Pulls objects toward each other (Earth pulls things down) |
| **Friction** | Slows motion when surfaces rub together |
| **Magnetism** | Attracts or repels certain metals |
| **Applied force** | A push or pull you apply |
| **Air resistance** | Friction from air (acts on moving objects) |

## Newton's First Law (Inertia)

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion, **unless acted on by an unbalanced force**.

This property is called **inertia** — objects resist changes to their motion.

> A soccer ball on the grass stays still until someone kicks it (applied force).
> A rolling ball eventually stops because friction (an unbalanced force) slows it down.

## Practice

1. When you stop pedaling a bike, it eventually slows down. What force causes this?
2. A book on a shelf isn't falling. What forces are balanced?
3. Push a toy car across a table. What unbalanced force makes it stop?
4. Why do seatbelts exist? (Think about inertia.)

## Remember

- Force = push or pull
- Balanced forces = no change in motion
- Unbalanced forces = change in motion
- Inertia: objects resist changes to their motion
