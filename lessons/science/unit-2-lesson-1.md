# The Water Cycle

## Learning Objectives
- Trace the path of water through the water cycle
- Identify the four main processes: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection
- Explain why the water cycle is essential for life on Earth

## The Four Stages

**1. Evaporation**
Heat from the sun turns liquid water into water vapor (gas). Water evaporates from oceans, lakes, rivers, and even from soil and plants.

> A puddle disappears on a sunny day — the water evaporated.

**2. Condensation**
Water vapor cools and turns back into tiny liquid droplets. These droplets form clouds.

> Fog on a cold mirror — your warm breath condensed on the cool surface.

**3. Precipitation**
When cloud droplets combine and become heavy enough, they fall as rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

**4. Collection (Runoff and Infiltration)**
Water flows into rivers, lakes, and oceans. Some seeps into the ground (infiltration) and becomes groundwater.

> Rivers flow to the ocean, where the cycle begins again.

## The Cycle Never Ends

The water cycle has no beginning or end. The same water on Earth today has been cycling for billions of years.

```
Sun heats water → Evaporation → Clouds form (Condensation)
                                      ↓
                              Precipitation
                                      ↓
                          Collection → Back to start
```

## Why It Matters

- Distributes fresh water across the planet
- Shapes weather patterns and climate
- Refills rivers, lakes, and groundwater
- Essential for all life on Earth

## Remember

- The sun drives the water cycle
- Evaporation → Condensation → Precipitation → Collection → repeat
- The same water keeps cycling — it's never "used up"
- Without the water cycle, there would be no fresh water on land
