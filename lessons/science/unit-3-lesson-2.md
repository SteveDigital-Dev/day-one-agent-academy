# Animal Adaptations

## Learning Objectives
- Define adaptation as a trait that helps an organism survive
- Distinguish between structural and behavioral adaptations
- Give examples of adaptations in different environments

## What Is an Adaptation?

An **adaptation** is a feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its environment. Adaptations develop over many generations through **natural selection**.

## Structural Adaptations

Physical features of an organism's body.

| Adaptation | Animal | Environment |
|------------|--------|-------------|
| Thick fur | Polar bear | Arctic |
| Long neck | Giraffe | Savanna (reach tall trees) |
| Camouflage coloring | Chameleon | Forest |
| Webbed feet | Duck | Water |
| Sharp claws | Eagle | Sky/trees (catching prey) |

## Behavioral Adaptations

Actions an organism takes to survive.

| Adaptation | Animal | Purpose |
|------------|--------|---------|
| Hibernation | Bear | Survive winter when food is scarce |
| Migration | Monarch butterfly | Move to warmer climate |
| Nocturnal activity | Owl | Hunt at night, avoid daytime predators |
| Herding | Zebra | Safety in numbers |

## Adaptations by Environment

**Desert:** Water conservation is key. Camels store fat in humps, not water. Cacti have thick skin and store water in stems.

**Arctic:** Staying warm. Polar bears have thick fur and fat layers. Arctic foxes have white fur for camouflage in snow.

**Ocean:** Moving through water. Fish have streamlined bodies. Whales have blubber for insulation.

## Practice

1. A camel can go weeks without water. Is this structural or behavioral?
2. Why do arctic animals tend to be white?
3. Name a structural and a behavioral adaptation of a bird.
4. What would happen to polar bears if the Arctic ice melted?

## Remember

- Adaptations help organisms survive in their environment
- Structural = body features; Behavioral = actions
- Adaptations develop over many generations, not within a single lifetime
- Environment determines which adaptations are advantageous
