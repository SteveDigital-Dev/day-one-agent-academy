# Energy: Types and Transformation

## Learning Objectives
- Identify different forms of energy
- Understand that energy can change from one form to another
- Explain the law of conservation of energy

## Forms of Energy

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Kinetic** | Energy of motion | A rolling ball, flowing water |
| **Potential** | Stored energy | A stretched rubber band, a book on a shelf |
| **Thermal** | Heat energy | A hot stove, body heat |
| **Chemical** | Stored in bonds of matter | Food, batteries, gasoline |
| **Electrical** | Energy from moving charges | Lightning, power outlets |
| **Light** | Electromagnetic energy | Sun, flashlight |
| **Sound** | Vibrations through matter | Music, talking |

## Energy Transformation

Energy is constantly changing from one form to another:

**Flashlight:** Chemical (battery) → Electrical → Light

**Car engine:** Chemical (gasoline) → Thermal → Kinetic (motion)

**Eating lunch:** Chemical (food) → Thermal + Kinetic (body heat and movement)

Nothing creates or destroys energy — it only changes form.

## Conservation of Energy

**Energy cannot be created or destroyed.** It can only be transferred or transformed.

> When you turn on a lamp: electrical energy from the outlet becomes light energy and thermal energy (heat). The total energy stays the same — some just becomes heat.

This is why your room gets warm when you leave the lights on — electrical energy is being converted to both light and heat.

## Remember

- Energy comes in many forms
- Energy is always transforming, never disappearing
- Chemical energy in food becomes the energy your body uses
- Total energy in a closed system stays constant
