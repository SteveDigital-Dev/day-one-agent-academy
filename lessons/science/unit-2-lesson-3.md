# Earth's Layers

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the three main layers of the Earth
- Describe the properties of the crust, mantle, and core
- Explain how Earth's structure relates to earthquakes and volcanoes

## The Three Main Layers

| Layer | Depth | Composition | Temperature |
|-------|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Crust** | 0–70 km | Solid rock | Cool at surface |
| **Mantle** | 70–2,900 km | Semi-solid rock | Very hot |
| **Core** | 2,900–6,371 km | Iron and nickel | Extremely hot (5,000°C+) |

The core is divided into the **outer core** (liquid iron) and **inner core** (solid iron, despite extreme heat, because of immense pressure).

## The Crust

The Earth's crust is thin like the skin of an apple. It comes in two types:

**Oceanic crust:** Thin (5–10 km), dense, made of basalt. Found under oceans.

**Continental crust:** Thick (30–70 km), less dense, made of granite. Found under continents.

> The crust is broken into large pieces called **tectonic plates** that float on the mantle and move slowly.

## The Mantle

The mantle is the thickest layer. It's made of hot, semi-solid rock that flows very slowly (like thick honey over millions of years).

This slow flowing motion is called **convection** — hot rock rises, cools, sinks, and heats up again. This convection is what moves the tectonic plates above.

## The Core

The **outer core** is liquid iron and nickel. Its movement generates Earth's **magnetic field**, which protects us from solar radiation.

The **inner core** is solid iron and nickel — kept solid by the extreme pressure of all the layers above it, despite temperatures similar to the sun's surface.

## Practice

1. Which layer is the thinnest? Which is the thickest?
2. Why is the inner core solid even though it's hotter than the outer core?
3. How does the mantle's convection relate to tectonic plates?
4. What would happen if the outer core stopped moving?

## Remember

- Crust (thin, solid) → Mantle (thick, semi-solid) → Core (liquid outer, solid inner)
- Convection in the mantle drives plate movement
- The outer core's motion creates Earth's magnetic field
- The crust is like an apple's skin compared to the whole fruit
