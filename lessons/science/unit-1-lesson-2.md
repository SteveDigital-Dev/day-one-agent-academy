# Properties of Materials

## Learning Objectives
- Identify physical properties of materials (color, hardness, flexibility)
- Classify materials as conductors or insulators
- Explain why different materials are used for different purposes

## Physical Properties

**Physical properties** can be observed or measured without changing what the material is.

| Property | What It Measures | Example |
|----------|-----------------|---------|
| **Hardness** | Resistance to scratching | Diamond is the hardest natural material |
| **Flexibility** | Ability to bend without breaking | Rubber is flexible; glass is not |
| **Transparency** | How much light passes through | Glass is transparent; wood is opaque |
| **Density** | Mass per unit volume | Iron is denser than water |
| **Conductivity** | Ability to transfer heat or energy | Copper conducts well; rubber does not |

## Conductors vs. Insulators

**Conductors** transfer heat and electricity easily.
> Metals (copper, aluminum, iron) are good conductors. That's why pots are made of metal — heat transfers from the stove to the food.

**Insulators** resist the transfer of heat and electricity.
> Rubber, plastic, wood, and air are insulators. That's why oven mitts are made of rubber or thick fabric — they protect your hands from heat.

## Why Material Properties Matter

Engineers choose materials based on properties:

- A bridge needs **strong, rigid** materials (steel)
- An oven mitt needs **heat-resistant** insulators (silicone)
- A window needs **transparent** material (glass)
- Electrical wires need **conductive** cores (copper) with **insulating** coating (rubber)

## Practice

1. Why are cooking pots made of metal but handles made of plastic or wood?
2. Which would be a better insulator: a metal water bottle or a plastic one? Why?
3. Name a material that is both flexible and waterproof.
4. Why might aluminum be used in an airplane but not in a bridge?

## Remember

- Physical properties can be observed without changing the material
- Conductors transfer heat/electricity; insulators block it
- Material choice depends on the job it needs to do
- Same material, different use: metal in a pot vs. metal in a wire
