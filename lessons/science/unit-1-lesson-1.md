# States of Matter

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the three states of matter: solid, liquid, gas
- Describe the properties of each state
- Explain how matter changes between states

## The Three States of Matter

Everything around you is made of **matter**. Matter exists in three states:

| State | Shape | Volume | Examples |
|-------|-------|--------|----------|
| **Solid** | Fixed shape | Fixed volume | Rock, ice, book |
| **Liquid** | Takes shape of container | Fixed volume | Water, milk, oil |
| **Gas** | No fixed shape | Fills any space | Air, steam, helium |

## What Makes Them Different?

The difference is how tightly packed the particles (atoms/molecules) are:

**Solids:** Particles are packed tightly together. They vibrate in place but don't move around. That's why solids keep their shape.

**Liquids:** Particles are close together but can slide past each other. That's why liquids flow and take the shape of their container.

**Gases:** Particles are far apart and move freely. That's why gases expand to fill any space.

## Changes of State

Matter can change state when heated or cooled:

```
Solid → (melt) → Liquid → (evaporate) → Gas
Gas → (condense) → Liquid → (freeze) → Solid
```

> Example: Ice (solid) melts into water (liquid), which evaporates into steam (gas).

The **temperature** at which a solid melts is its **melting point**. Water's melting point is 0°C (32°F).

## Practice

1. Name three things that are liquids at room temperature.
2. What happens to water when you heat it to 100°C?
3. Why does ice float on water? (Hint: think about how particles are arranged.)
4. Is butter a solid or liquid at room temperature? What about when melted?

## Remember

- Solids have a fixed shape; liquids take their container's shape; gases fill any space
- Heating causes melting and evaporation; cooling causes freezing and condensation
- All matter is made of particles — the arrangement determines the state
