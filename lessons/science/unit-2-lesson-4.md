# Rock Types and the Rock Cycle

## Learning Objectives
- Identify the three main types of rocks
- Explain how each type forms
- Trace the path of the rock cycle

## Three Types of Rocks

**Igneous** — Formed from cooled magma or lava.
> When a volcano erupts and the lava cools, it becomes igneous rock.
> Examples: granite, basalt, obsidian

**Sedimentary** — Formed from compressed layers of sediment (sand, mud, organic material).
> Sand at the bottom of a lake gets compressed over millions of years into sandstone.
> Examples: sandstone, limestone, shale

**Metamorphic** — Formed when existing rocks are changed by heat and pressure.
> Granite subjected to extreme heat and pressure becomes gneiss.
> Examples: marble (from limestone), slate (from shale)

## The Rock Cycle

Rocks don't stay one type forever. They change through the **rock cycle**:

```
Igneous → (weathering/erosion) → Sediment → (compaction) → Sedimentary
Sedimentary → (heat/pressure) → Metamorphic → (melting) → Magma → (cooling) → Igneous
```

Any rock type can become any other rock type given the right conditions.

**Key processes:**
- **Weathering & erosion:** Breaks rocks into smaller pieces
- **Compaction & cementation:** Presses sediment into rock
- **Heat & pressure:** Changes rock's structure
- **Melting:** Turns rock into magma

## Practice

1. A volcano erupts and the lava cools on the surface. What type of rock forms?
2. Layers of sand build up on the ocean floor over millions of years. What type of rock forms?
3. A piece of shale is buried deep underground and subjected to heat. What might it become?
4. Can an igneous rock become sedimentary? Describe the process.

## Remember

- Igneous = cooled magma/lava
- Sedimentary = compressed sediment layers
- Metamorphic = changed by heat and pressure
- The rock cycle means no rock type is permanent
