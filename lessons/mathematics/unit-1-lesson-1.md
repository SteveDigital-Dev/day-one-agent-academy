# Understanding Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Define a fraction as a part of a whole
- Identify numerator and denominator
- Represent fractions using visual models

## What Is a Fraction?

A **fraction** tells us how many parts we have of something that is divided into equal parts.

Every fraction has two parts:
- The **numerator** (top number) — how many parts we have
- The **denominator** (bottom number) — how many equal parts the whole is divided into

> Example: In the fraction 3/4, the numerator is 3 and the denominator is 4. This means 3 out of 4 equal parts.

## Visualizing Fractions

Imagine a pizza cut into 8 equal slices:
- If you eat 1 slice, you ate **1/8** of the pizza
- If you eat 3 slices, you ate **3/8** of the pizza
- If you eat all 8 slices, you ate **8/8** = 1 whole pizza

## Key Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Numerator** | The top number — how many parts we have |
| **Denominator** | The bottom number — total equal parts |
| **Whole** | The complete thing being divided |
| **Unit fraction** | A fraction with 1 as the numerator (1/2, 1/3, 1/4) |

## Practice

1. Write the fraction that represents 5 parts out of 7 equal parts.
2. In the fraction 2/9, what is the numerator? What is the denominator?
3. Draw a circle divided into 6 equal parts. Shade 4 parts. Write the fraction.

## Remember

- The denominator can never be zero
- A fraction less than 1 (like 3/4) is called a **proper fraction**
- A fraction greater than or equal to 1 (like 5/3) is called an **improper fraction**
