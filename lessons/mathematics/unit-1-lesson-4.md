# Comparing and Ordering Fractions

## Learning Objectives
- Compare fractions with like and unlike denominators
- Order fractions from least to greatest (and greatest to least)
- Use benchmark fractions to estimate

## Comparing Fractions with Like Denominators

When denominators are the same, the fraction with the **larger numerator** is greater.

> Example: 5/8 > 3/8 (five eighths is more than three eighths)

## Comparing Fractions with Unlike Denominators

When denominators differ, you need a **common denominator**.

**Step 1:** Find the least common denominator (LCD)
**Step 2:** Convert both fractions
**Step 3:** Compare the numerators

> Example: Compare 2/3 and 3/5
> - LCD of 3 and 5 is 15
> - 2/3 = 10/15
> - 3/5 = 9/15
> - 10/15 > 9/15, so **2/3 > 3/5**

## Using Benchmark Fractions

Quick benchmarks to remember:
- **1/2** is the midpoint
- Anything greater than 1/2 is closer to 1
- Anything less than 1/2 is closer to 0

> Example: Is 3/8 greater or less than 1/2?
> 1/2 = 4/8, and 3/8 < 4/8, so 3/8 is **less than** 1/2.

## Practice

1. Which is greater: 4/7 or 3/7?
2. Which is greater: 1/3 or 2/5?
3. Order from least to greatest: 3/4, 1/2, 2/3
4. True or false: 5/6 > 7/8

## Remember

- Same denominator → compare numerators directly
- Different denominator → find LCD first
- Benchmark: 1/2 is your friend for quick estimates
