# Division as Sharing and Grouping

## Learning Objectives
- Understand division as equal sharing and equal grouping
- Relate division to multiplication
- Use fact families to solve division problems

## Two Ways to Think About Division

**Sharing (partitive):** Split into equal groups.
> 12 cookies shared among 3 people → 12 ÷ 3 = 4 cookies each

**Grouping (measurement):** How many groups of a size?
> 12 cookies, 3 per bag → 12 ÷ 3 = 4 bags

Both questions give the same answer: **12 ÷ 3 = 4**

## The Division-Multiplication Connection

Division is the **inverse** of multiplication. They undo each other.

If 6 × 7 = 42, then:
- 42 ÷ 6 = 7
- 42 ÷ 7 = 6

These four facts form a **fact family**: 6, 7, and 42.

## Using Fact Families

To solve 56 ÷ 8, ask: "What times 8 equals 56?"
Since 8 × 7 = 56, then 56 ÷ 8 = 7.

## Practice

1. 24 ÷ 6 = ?

3. Write the fact family for 4, 9, and 36
4. A teacher has 35 pencils to give equally to 5 students. How many does each student get?

## Remember

- Division undoes multiplication
- Use multiplication facts to solve division problems
- Both sharing and grouping give the same answer
